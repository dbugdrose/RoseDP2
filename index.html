Mary Oliver (1935-2019)


During her lifetime, Mary Oliver was a prolific poet and the recipient of numerous literary honors. Over the course of her career, Oliver wrote 55 collections of poetry and nonfiction prose and amassed 12 literary awards, including the Pulitzer Prize and National Book Award. Her work focuses on nature and her reverence for the world around us. Oliver expertly conveys the delight she finds within the simplicity of the mundane.

Her deep appreciation for nature seen throughout her work was inspired by her forest exploration during her childhood in Maple Heights, Ohio. These adventures in wilderness, in conjunction with a traumatic family life, prompted her to develop a rich and beautiful inner world as a form of escapism. She began translating these inner experiences into written word at the tender age of 14, and she continued to do so up until her death in 2019.

The natural elements in Oliver’s poetry, love interwoven throughout all her works, and her gentle, devoted tones have allowed her works to resonate with a vast audience, earning her the title of best-selling poet in 2007. Her poems hold immense significance to me as well. During times of darkness, they prompt me to find beauty in the little things. When all feels lost, they serve as reminders that joy exists wherever you choose to see it. Though she is no longer with us, Mary Oliver is immortalized by her poetry and the meaning it carries for her readers.
